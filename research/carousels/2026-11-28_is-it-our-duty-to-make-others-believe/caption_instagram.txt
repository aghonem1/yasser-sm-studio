Even the Prophet didn't control hearts. So is it our duty to make others believe? The Mirror answers with two verses.

The last reflection from The Mirror showed how little of another person we are able to see. The next section of Chapter Two turns to what we have actually been asked to do.

It begins with the duty of the Messenger himself. A verse of Surah al-Ra‘d (13:40) describes that duty as delivering the message, and leaves the account of each person to God. He was not required to force it into people's hearts, to search their souls, or to pronounce their final judgement.

"So remind them; you are only one who reminds. You are not appointed to control them." (Surah al-Ghashiyah, 88:21–22)

A person who reminds points towards the truth, but does not force anyone towards it. He speaks to the mind and to the heart, and makes no attempt to take control of either.

If the Prophet was not appointed to control people's faith, how can anyone after him claim authority over their consciences?
