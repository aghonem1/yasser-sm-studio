How should we weigh differing opinions in Islam? Start with the Qur'an. Don't start with an idea and search it for support.

That's the method Hold Firmly sets out before its first chapter. Scholars, thinkers and jurists are respected. Their work is human effort that may bring great benefit, and it stays subject to the Book of Allah, never above it. The authentic Sunnah is the practical explanation of the Qur'an.

Words take their value from how far they agree with revelation. Not from the speaker's reputation. Not from applause.

The Qur'an never addresses a school, a sect or a group. It addresses one Ummah, whom Allah Himself named Muslims. So the first question isn't what Muslims have differed about. It's what Allah united them upon.

Legitimate scholarly disagreement isn't the problem. The problem begins when it turns into fanaticism, partisanship or destructive dispute.

And the author doesn't ask for agreement. He asks you to weigh every idea against the Book of Allah, his own included. If he has erred, the Book of Allah is more deserving to be followed.
