The last reflection from The Mirror showed how little of another person we are able to see. The next section of Chapter Two turns from what we cannot know to what we have actually been asked to do.

It begins with the duty of the Messenger himself. The chapter points to a verse of Surah al-Ra‘d (13:40) that describes that duty as delivering the message, and leaves the account of each person to God. The Prophet, the book says, was to deliver God’s message clearly. He was not required to force it into people’s hearts, to search their souls, or to pronounce their final judgement.

A second verse follows, from Surah al-Ghashiyah:

“So remind them; you are only one who reminds. You are not appointed to control them.” — Surah al-Ghashiyah, 88:21–22

The book then draws out what it means to remind. A person who reminds points towards the truth, but does not force anyone towards it. He speaks to the mind and to the heart, and makes no attempt to take control of either.

The section ends with a question that turns back on the reader. If the Prophet was not appointed to control people’s faith, how can anyone after him claim authority over their consciences?

This is a quiet correction for anyone who speaks to others about faith. The task the chapter describes is real, and it asks for care: to make the message clear. But it stops where another person’s heart begins. What happens there, and the reckoning that follows, the chapter leaves with God.

### To sit with

- When I remind someone, am I pointing towards the truth, or trying to take hold of their heart?
