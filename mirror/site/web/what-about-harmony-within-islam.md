Hold Firmly began with a message. A friend had nearly finished The Mirror and wrote to say it would help Muslims treat people of other faiths fairly. Then he added a line the author could not put down: “But what about harmony within Islam?”

The opening pages describe what the author did next, and what he chose not to do. He did not start with history, with the disputes themselves, or with a defence of one opinion against another. He went back to the Qur’an, and he is careful about how. The aim was not to hunt for verses that supported a view he already held. It was to ask what Allah wants the community to be built upon.

That changed the question. Instead of “Who is right and who is wrong?” or “Which school is closest to the truth?”, the book asks how Allah Himself describes those who believe in Him. The answer it finds is simple. The Qur’an addresses the believers together, gives them one name, and commands them all to hold to one rope.

“Hold firmly to the Rope of Allah, all together, and do not become divided.” (Qur’an 3:103)

From there the introduction makes the claim that shapes every later chapter: “the path to harmony does not begin by eliminating differences.” It begins by returning to what Allah united us upon, before we become absorbed in what we differ over.

The book is clear about what it is not. It does not propose a new school of thought. It does not ask anyone to abandon the existing ones or to think less of sincere scholarship. Differences of opinion existed among the earliest Muslims and did not stop them being one community. The call is to put things back in order: Islam as the highest identity, brotherhood as the first relationship, and the Qur’an as the reference against which every opinion is weighed.

### To sit with

- When I meet another Muslim, which do I reach for first: what we share, or where we differ?

- Is there a disagreement in my life that has grown bigger than the bond it sits inside?
