# How can wudu prepare the heart for prayer?

The third chapter of My Prayer follows the water from limb to limb and asks what each one has been doing.

Yasser Fares · 27 November 2026 · 2 minute read ·

After the call and the intention comes water. The third chapter of My Prayer calls water a visible mercy. As the believer approaches it, the book says, wudu can be received as a movement from the dust of the road towards the clarity of standing before Allah, and from scattered senses towards a gathered heart.

The chapter is careful about what it offers. The meanings it attaches to each limb are not special prayers to recite while washing, nor alternatives to the known rulings of wudu. They are pauses for reflection, so that the water passes over the heart as it passes over the body.

Then it goes limb by limb. The hands were created to work, build and give, so washing them is a moment to remember what they have done. The mouth is being prepared for recitation, and a mouth that will read al-Fatihah should not willingly backbite, lie or wound. The face will soon rest on the earth in sujud, and a face that prostrates to its Lord should not rise in arrogance over His servants. At the arms the meaning of strength is renewed: it should support truth rather than oppression. The head bows in worship to none but Allah, and the touch of water calls the mind away from pride and suspicion. At the feet, the chapter asks about the roads they walk.

“Where am I going, and does this road bring me closer to the destination for which I ask in al-Fatihah?”

In this way, the book says, wudu gathers the whole person. It also names a test. The meaning of water is incomplete within us if nothing of it appears in our dealings. The one who washes to speak with Allah should return to His creation with hands further from harm, a tongue further from wounding, and a heart quicker to mercy.

### To sit with

- At my next wudu, can I slow down at one limb and remember what it has done today?
