# How does temptation begin?

The second chapter of The Evil I finds the start of Adam’s fall a moment before his hand reached the tree.

Yasser Fares · 26 November 2026 · 1 minute read ·

The second chapter of The Evil I goes back to Adam and the tree. Humanity did not begin to fall, the book says, when Adam stretched out his hand. It began a moment before, when the question inside his heart changed.

Before the whisper, Adam’s heart was not occupied with what it desired. It rested on what Allah desired for him. The chapter calls that the true Paradise: not merely a place, but a state of the heart, at peace because it had entrusted its choice to its Lord.

Satan knew that a heart like that does not openly embrace rebellion, so he never began by inviting Adam to disobey. He redirected Adam’s gaze. The chapter gives the whisper as the Qur’an records it (Surah Ta-Ha, verse 120).

“O Adam, shall I lead you to the Tree of Eternity and to a kingdom that never fades away?”

The book asks us to notice what is missing. There is no call to disobey, only the offer of something better. Eternity comes first, then a kingdom, and everything in the offer turns on the self: what it will gain, possess and become. Temptation, the chapter says, rarely begins by pulling us away from Allah. It begins by drawing our attention towards ourselves.

The book is careful about desire. Wanting was not the sin, because desire is part of the nature Allah created within us. The danger came when desire grew louder than obedience. Servitude is tested at exactly that point: not when Allah’s command agrees with our wishes, but when it asks us to rise above them.

So Adam’s struggle, the chapter says, was never with a tree. The tree did not come searching for him, and its fruit did not call his name. His struggle was with himself.

### To sit with

- Where today has “What do I want?” grown louder in me than what Allah has chosen for me?
