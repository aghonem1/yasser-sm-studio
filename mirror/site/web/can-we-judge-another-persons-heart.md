# Can we judge what is in another person’s heart?

The second chapter of The Mirror begins with how little of another person we are actually able to see.

Yasser Fares · 24 November 2026 · 1 minute read ·

The second chapter of The Mirror starts from something simple. Every person has a face that others can see, and a heart that only God knows completely. Actions can be watched and words can be heard. The intentions, struggles and questions behind them stay out of our sight.

The book measures the gap: people see one moment, while God sees the entire journey. He knows what reached a person and what never did, what was grasped and what was misread, what the heart took in and what it turned away. Then the chapter asks its question.

“How, then, can someone who has read only one page pass judgement upon an entire book?”

This, the chapter says, is why Islam rests our dealings with people on what they visibly do, and leaves the final judgement of belief and intention to God.

The first section shows how small our one page is. People do not start from the same family, culture or circumstances, and the truth does not arrive to everyone in the same shape. One person comes to know Islam through a Muslim whose honesty and mercy show its beauty. Another meets it only through anger, injustice or disturbing images. One reads the Qur’an in a language he understands. Another is handed fragments, poor translations or misleading accounts.

So we cannot tell, the book says, how much light reached someone, or how much smoke stood in its way. A person who seems far from faith today may be searching for it quietly. A person we have counted as lost may, in God’s sight, be moving towards the truth.

The section ends where the chapter began. The heart travels by paths our eyes cannot follow, and God alone sees each step.

### To sit with

- Whom have I summed up from a single page, and how much of their road do I not know?
