# Allahu Akbar — greater than what?

My Prayer starts before the prayer does, with two words most of us have heard thousands of times.

Yasser Fares · 15 November 2026 · 1 minute read ·

Before the prayer there is the call. My Prayer starts there, and its first chapter spends its opening pages on two words most of us have heard thousands of times.

The adhan could have said “Allah is great”, and that would have been true. It says Allahu Akbar, Allah is greater, and leaves the comparison unfinished. Greater than what? The book’s answer is that the sentence is left open on purpose, because every listener completes it differently: the work that has swallowed the day, the money one is afraid to lose, the sleep, the grief, the fear. Whatever has grown large enough to crowd the heart goes into the blank, and the verdict on it is always the same.

The chapter is careful not to turn this into contempt for ordinary life. Work is a trust. Family is a trust. Wealth is a blessing.

“Allahu Akbar is not an invitation to despise the world.”

What the call asks is only that a means should not become the final end.

The chapter also notices the repetition. The takbir comes four times at the start of the adhan, and the book reads this as patience with a scattered heart, which rarely returns at the first knock. It hears and wakes. It hears again and remembers. It reorders its priorities, and then it gets up.

The rest of the adhan follows the same logic. The testimonies fill the space the takbir has cleared. “Come to prayer” asks the heart to arrive before the feet do. And the call does not promise comfort. It says falah, which the book describes as a flourishing that joins soundness in this life with a good end.

When the takbir settles, the chapter says, “everything recovers its proper size.”

### To sit with

- What would I put in the blank today: greater than what?

- At the next adhan, can I let the heart arrive before the feet?
