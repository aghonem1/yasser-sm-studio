# Two verses, one movement towards repair

The Language of Marital Repair starts by refusing to read Surah al-Nisa 4:34 on its own.

Yasser Fares · 15 November 2026 · 2 minute read ·

Few verses are quoted as often, or as partially, as Surah al-Nisa 4:34. The Language of Marital Repair starts by refusing to read it alone.

Chapter One sets verses 34 and 35 side by side and argues that they form one continuous passage. It first records the prevailing reading fairly. The commentators generally understood verse 34 as dealing with the husband’s responsibility within marriage and with nushūz, and verse 35 as dealing with shiqāq, a breach the couple can no longer mend by themselves. Most read the measures in verse 34 as graduated steps.

Then the chapter follows the text past the point where many quotations stop. Verse 34 does not end with its measures. It continues: “But if they obey you, then seek no way against them” (4:34), and it closes by recalling that God is Most High, Supremely Great. The book reads both as limits. Whatever is done in response to a disruption must stop when the disruption ends, and to continue beyond that point is transgression. And no spouse’s responsibility amounts to unrestricted power, because it remains subject to God’s authority and judgement.

Verse 35 carries the same line of thought forward. If a breach is feared, an arbiter is appointed from his family and one from hers: “If they both desire reconciliation, God will bring harmony between them” (4:35).

Read together, the chapter concludes, the two verses trace a single process: responsibility, the emergence of disruption, graduated efforts to restore the relationship, a required stopping point, and outside help if the distance widens. Its direction is stated without hedging.

“The sequence is directed towards restoration, not punishment for its own sake.”

This becomes the test the rest of the book applies. If a reading of any one expression breaks that progression, or turns a remedial step into an instrument of injury, coercion or revenge, the book says its fit with the context has to be reconsidered. Chapter One does not yet settle how each term should be rendered. That work is done root by root in the chapters that follow.

### To sit with

- When I quote a verse, do I read on to where it ends?

- In a conflict at home, am I looking for repair, or for a way to win?
