Before its first chapter, Hold Firmly sets out its method, and it starts by saying what the book is not. It is not a history of Muslim disagreements, a comparison of the schools of thought, or a ruling on whose juristic or theological opinions are stronger.

What it offers instead is a return to the Qur’an, so that we might see the Ummah as its Lord sees it. The work of scholars, thinkers and jurists is treated with respect, as human effort that may bring great benefit and that remains subject to the Book of Allah, never above it. The authentic Sunnah is named as the practical explanation of the Qur’an. So the book does not begin with an idea and then search the Qur’an for support. It begins with the Qur’an and asks what Allah wants from us.

The method rests on one line, which the book gives as Allah’s own declaration.

“The Word of Allah is supreme.”

Human beings, the author writes, neither create this reality nor diminish it. Words take their value from how far they agree with revelation, not from the reputation of the speaker or the number of people applauding.

Two things follow. The first is a change of question. The Qur’an never addresses a school, a sect or a group. It addresses one Ummah, to whom Allah Himself gave the name Muslims. So the book’s first question is not what Muslims have differed about, but what Allah united them upon. Legitimate scholarly disagreement is not treated as the problem. The problem begins when it turns into fanaticism, partisanship or destructive dispute.

The second concerns the reader. The author does not ask for agreement. He asks each reader to weigh every idea against the Book of Allah, including the ideas in his own pages. If he has erred, he writes, the Book of Allah is more deserving to be followed.

### To sit with

- When I hold an opinion firmly, did I weigh it against the Qur’an, or go to the Qur’an looking for support?

Drawn from Hold Firmly: “Methodology”, the statement of method between the introduction and Chapter One.
