# Is the self our greatest enemy?

The Evil I begins by asking whether the way we usually talk about the self is the way the Qur’an talks about it.

Yasser Fares · 15 November 2026 · 2 minute read ·

We have all heard it said: the self is the enemy, the source of every evil, the thing to be crushed. The Evil I begins by asking whether that is how the Qur’an speaks.

The author describes gathering every Qur’anic passage about the nafs and finding that much of our everyday religious language does not match it. The Qur’an, he notes, introduces the soul with an oath: “By the soul and the One who fashioned it” (Surah al-Shams, 91:7). If the self were evil by nature, why would Allah swear by it, and why speak first of how well He fashioned it?

The preface gives its answer plainly.

“The problem has never been the self itself. The problem is the direction in which it turns.”

A self turned towards its Lord is purified. A self turned towards desire clouds over. It is neither pure evil nor automatic goodness. It is created, honoured, entrusted with responsibility and asked to choose.

Chapter One then looks at where things first went wrong, and finds that the first disobedience did not begin with a hand or a foot. It began with a word: “I”. Iblis did not deny that Allah created him, and he did not doubt His existence. He looked at himself, then at Adam, and judged them both: “I am better than him” (Surah al-A‘raf, 7:12). His ruin, the book says, was making that “I” the measure by which he judged the command of God.

The word itself is not the problem. Prophets and the righteous said it too. The book’s question is which “I” is speaking. Its aim is not to destroy the ego. It is to free it from the illusion that it stands independent of God, until the heart stops asking “What do I want?” and starts asking “What does God want from me?”

### To sit with

- Where in my day does “I” quietly become the measure of what is right?

- Do I treat my self as an enemy to be hated, or as a trust to be turned in the right direction?

Drawn from The Evil I: Author’s Preface and Chapter One, “The First ‘I’”.
